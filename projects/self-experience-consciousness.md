## WHY THIS RESEARCH EXISTS

The conversation began with synthetic training data and its reliability. SYN-01 then asked whether independently verified lessons from prior mistakes improve performance on unseen tasks beyond ordinary memory/retrieval. The next question was deeper: can experience causally change an artificial system's self-model, stable behavior and individual trajectory? Finally: what, if anything, could these findings tell us about subjective experience and the first-person “I”?

This is a research question, not a claim that consciousness has been implemented, measured or disproven.

## RESEARCH QUESTIONS

1. How does a system distinguish self-generated actions from external events?
2. Does a verified history improve calibrated predictions of its own capabilities and errors?
3. Can different histories produce stable, causally explainable behavioral individuality?
4. How does an agent form useful questions and revise beliefs without merely replaying pending tasks?
5. What would discriminate between competing theories of consciousness, and what remains untestable in a synthetic setup?

## EPISTEMIC BOUNDARIES

- Functional competence ≠ causal mechanism ≠ subjective experience.
- SELF-CONTENT ≠ SUBJECT-POLE; MEMORY CONTENT ≠ MINENESS.

- Retrieval ≠ learning of model weights; evidence ≠ truth; experiment design ≠ experiment result.

## SELF-CAUSAL-01 — FALSIFIABLE CANDIDATE

**Hypothesis:** Under equal budgets, a system with an updated, independently checked model of its own capabilities and errors predicts and corrects future errors better than (A) ordinary history alone or (B) history plus a static self-description. Targeted corruption/removal of the self-model should predictably affect outcomes.

**Null/alternatives:** No incremental effect, or effect explained by retrieval, context length, prompt scaffolding, seed, data leakage or extra computation.

**Controls:** A history only; B history + self-description; C history + verified updated self-model; holdouts, equal budgets, independent assessment, ablations, leakage checks, repeat trials. No results obtained yet.

## EXPERIMENT CATALOG — PROPOSED, NOT EXECUTED

1. SELF-MODEL: own-error prediction, calibration and ablation.
2. EXPERIENCE/IDENTITY: identical agents with divergent verified histories.
3. SELF/OTHER: attribution of own versus external changes.
4. INITIATIVE: question formation versus planner/retrieval baselines.
5. BELIEF REVISION: evidence conflicts, uncertainty and supersession.
6. CONTINUITY: cross-session state and unselected alternatives.
7. GOALS/VALUES: origin and revision of bounded synthetic goals.
8. EMOTION-LIKE REGULATION: functional salience/uncertainty variables, not felt emotions.
9. EMBODIMENT: virtual body and sensorimotor feedback.
10. THEORY DISCRIMINATION: operational predictions of competing consciousness theories, only where applicable.
11. SYNTHETIC WORLDS: causal, social, resource-constrained and changing environments.
12. ADVERSARIAL SELF-REPORT: susceptibility of feeling/identity claims to prompting.

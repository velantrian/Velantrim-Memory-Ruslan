## Independent Russian translation comparison and reuse terms

Quran-for-all identifies its second Russian source as “Russian Османов” and displays the complete Al-Fatihah plus Al-Baqarah text in its chapter pages: https://quran-for-all.com/translations/ru_osmanov/1 and https://quran-for-all.com/translations/ru_osmanov/2. Its index separates named translators (including Кулиев and Османов) and identifies the target pages as Al-Fatihah (7 ayat) and Al-Baqarah (286 ayat). Directly checked comparison points include 1:4 (Kuliev “Day of retribution” vs Osmanov “Day of Judgment”), 2:3 (Kuliev “намаз” vs Osmanov’s prayer wording and extra explanatory language), 2:5 (Kuliev “successful” vs Osmanov’s bracketed otherworldly bliss), 2:14–15 (the “devils”/shayatin referent and “mocking” language), 2:17–20 (fire and storm are framed by the contiguous parable), 2:26 (Kuliev renders the example as a mosquito “or what is greater,” while Osmanov says “even what is smaller”; this is a material translation divergence and no single wording is adopted), 2:27 (covenant and severed connection), and 2:28–29 (death/resurrection and creation). These are translator comparisons, not a full independent Arabic philology audit.

Quran.com’s own Terms and Conditions, opened at https://quran.com/terms-and-conditions, state in sections 1.4, 2.2 and 2.3 that its content is for personal, non-commercial use and that copying, reproduction, derivative works, distribution, sale or public display beyond personal non-commercial use requires prior written consent. The v0.4 book/register will therefore paraphrase rather than reproduce long translation passages. Commercial-print reuse of any translated wording is not cleared by this research and requires separate rights review.

Quran-for-all’s page footer identifies the site as “Quran For All V6.2 developing by Nwahy.com” and includes “Copyright - The Noble Quran”; no translation-reuse license or permission for the Osmanov rendering was established from the opened pages. The project should not reproduce its long translation passages absent permission.

The system Noto Serif and Noto Sans fonts are installed for Cyrillic and the local Noto Naskh Arabic face is available for any short Arabic in the editable register. Font license notices still need to be confirmed in the final build inventory. The inherited visual assets are the project’s previous edition artwork; their files do not carry a reliable generator/license manifest, so the final source report will state this limitation and classify them as editorial illustrations, not documentary images.


## Additional source-use terms

The Quran Foundation Developer Terms (updated 2026-10-11) define API-returned scripture, translations, metadata and derived material as QF Content/Derived Materials. The terms allow display in an end-user application under conditions, prohibit selling/sublicensing/redistributing raw content, and require a separate signed commercial license before selling or redistributing QF Content; summaries that permit reconstruction remain subject to restrictions. These developer terms govern use of the Foundation APIs, not automatically every use of a webpage, but they reinforce that an offline commercial book cannot assume reuse rights. The pilot accessed public pages rather than an API and stores original paraphrases, citations, and short linguistic labels—not full translated text. Public/commercial publication remains rights-uncleared. https://api-docs.quran.foundation/legal/developer-terms/

The Quranic Arabic Corpus download page states its morphological annotation file is GPL-licensed and gives separate use terms: indicate the Corpus as source and link back; include copyright notice in verbatim copies or works containing a substantial portion; its annotation file is not to be changed when distributed verbatim. This project only cites individual records and uses the Corpus as a linguistic cross-check; it does not copy or redistribute the dataset. https://corpus.quran.com/download and https://corpus.quran.com/license.jsp


## Direct Russian translation checks for the six control cases

On 13 October 2026, the Quran.com Arabic display and its named Russian translation by Elmir Kuliev were opened directly for 2:177, 4:135, 5:32, 5:89, 49:6, and each verse 49:11–13. The relevant direct URLs are recorded in the v0.4 source catalog and unit register. The control dossiers also use the parallel sources cited by the completed six-case research workflow (Quranic Arabic Corpus lexical/morphological pages and identified tafsir pages); the Russian Kuliev rendering is not treated as the only possible interpretation. The 2:177 rendering “during battle” is therefore retained as a variant, not silently converted into an E07 exclusion. The Quran.com 5:89 Russian page has a visible concatenation typo in one phrase in its current rendering; this is noted as a source-page erratum and is not copied into the book. The range URL for 49:11–13 exposed 49:11, so verses 49:12 and 49:13 were separately opened to verify the rest of the Russian rendering.
